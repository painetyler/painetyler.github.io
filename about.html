I am a Research Scientist at MIT working in the Marine Autonomy Lab led by Dr. Mike Benjamin. I develop mathematical models of environments and autonomy, analyze their properties, and build robots that use them to maximize performance. In my work I use a wide range of theoretical concepts in control, estimation, and autonomy to solve problems in marine robotics such as multi-vehicle adaptive sampling, multi-agent competitions/games, and control of highly modified vehicles. More broadly, my research interests include multi-robot systems, control, estimation, and getting things to actually work in the field. Before coming to MIT, I was a machine design engineer at the Naval Undersea Warfare Center Division Keyport (NUWC) Keyport. If you are interested to learn more about my research check out the Research page.

Background

I have a PhD in Mechanical Engineering (MechE) and Oceanographic Engineering from MIT and WHOI through the Joint Program (2025), a MS in MechE from Johns Hopkins (2018), and a BS in MechE from Montana State (2011). I was a SMART scholar, and received the MIT Presidential Fellowship. I am a licensed professional engineer with about seven years of experience as a working machine design engineer.
