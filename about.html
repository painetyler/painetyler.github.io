Under Renovation (April 3, 2026)

I am a Research Scientist at MIT working in the Marine Autonomy Lab, led by Dr. Mike Benjamin. I develop mathematical models of environments and autonomy, analyze their properties, and build robots that use these insights to maximize performance. In my work, I draw on a wide range of theoretical concepts in control, estimation, and autonomy to solve problems in marine robotics including multi-vehicle adaptive sampling, multi-agent competitions and games, and control of highly modified vehicles. More broadly, my research interests include multi-robot systems, control, estimation, and getting things to reliably work in the field. Before coming to MIT, I was a machine design engineer at the Naval Undersea Warfare Center (NUWC) Keyport. If you would like to learn more about my research, please visit the Research page.

Background

I have a PhD in Mechanical Engineering (MechE) and Oceanographic Engineering from MIT and WHOI through the Joint Program (2025), a MS in MechE from Johns Hopkins (2018), and a BS in MechE from Montana State (2011). I was a SMART Scholar and received the MIT Presidential Fellowship. I am also a licensed Professional Engineer (PE) with approximately seven years of experience as a machine design engineer.

Other software: Ongoing research areas and licensable code

  • Multi-agent autonomy modules called moos-ivp-multi, a collection of atomic baseline algorithms for distributed and decentralized autonomy. These capabilities can be combined in different ways to generate sophisticated multi-agent systems that are more versatile than any single approach. They are layered on top of the open-source MOOS-IvP codebase and are licensable through MIT TLO.
  • High-performance controllers for marine vehicles, including work on the DARPA LINC program. Separately, we developed a controller to operate a Safe 29 Defender-class vessel at speeds exceeding 30 knots.
  • Adaptive sampling for marine carbon dioxide removal (mCDR) validation. As of Spring 2026, this is a new area of work that I plan to open source soon.
